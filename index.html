01 · Who we are

Named after the falcon that sees everything.

In Egyptian mythology, Horus is the falcon whose eye sees what others miss. That is our ambition as investors: to look through the index, understand what really drives returns, and act on evidence rather than noise.

Horus Asset Management was founded in Paris in 2026 by five students of the SKEMA Business School MSc in Financial Markets & Investments. We combine academic research, rigorous testing and simple rules that a client can understand in five minutes.

We start with one flagship strategy, built and tested over twenty years of data. We intend to grow from there, one well-tested idea at a time.

02 · Investment philosophy

Three sentences. One discipline.

Most investors hold a 60/40: 60% equities for growth, 40% bonds for safety. It earned 6.1% a year since 2007 and lost 36% in 2008–09. We keep the split, and change what fills it.

I.

Our factors are strategic.

We believe in three equity factors, small cap, growth and momentum, and we never switch them, even when another style has a good year.

II.

Our selection is tactical.

Every January we pick the ETFs that carry those factors best today, measured with the official Carhart four-factor model.

III.

Our 60/40 never changes.

No market timing. The split between equities and bonds is permanent, so the client always knows the risk they own.

03 · Flagship strategy

Horus Smart Beta 60/40: a 60/40 that owns the right kind of equities.

A market index owns every stock in proportion to its size. Decades of research show that some kinds of stocks have paid more than others. We build the equity half around three of them.

The equity half holds two ETFs at 30% each, chosen every January for their exposure to our three factors. The bond half holds two ETFs at 20% each, chosen for their three-year Sharpe ratio. Four funds, one rule, once a year.

04 · Investment process

Every January, four steps.

The factors stay the same; the funds that carry them change. The whole process fits in a spreadsheet, and every number can be recomputed.

I

Measure

One Carhart regression per equity ETF on the last 36 months: how much SMB, HML and MOM it holds today.

II

Score

z(SMB) + z(−HML) + z(MOM): one point per standard deviation above the average ETF. The three count equally.

III

Select

The Solver buys the two best-scored equity ETFs and the two bond ETFs with the best 3-year Sharpe ratio.

IV

Hold

We keep the four funds for twelve months, then start again. Low turnover, low cost.

The 60/40 is permanent: in 2008 and 2022 our equity half fell with the market. We cut losses; we do not avoid them.

12 · Insights

What we tested, and what we learned.

RESEARCH NOTE · TIMING

Why we kept the 60/40 permanent

We tested a monthly trend rule that cuts equities to 30% when the 60/40 trails cash.

It reduced the worst loss from −22.6% to −16.3%, but lowered the return from 9.0% to 8.4%, the information ratio from 0.54 to 0.33, and the Sharpe ratio of the last ten years from 0.70 to 0.65. It missed the rebounds of 2009, 2019 and 2020. We keep it as an option for more cautious mandates.

RESEARCH NOTE · FACTORS

Three factors beat each one alone

Each factor works on its own, but each one alone suffers falls of 26 to 29%.

Small cap alone: Sharpe 0.52, worst loss −26.1%. Growth alone: 0.71 and −29.2%. Momentum alone: 0.58 and −29.5%. Together they point to different funds in different years, and the worst loss shrinks to −22.6% with a Sharpe ratio of 0.69.

RESEARCH NOTE · CALENDAR

January or October?

We tested rebalancing every October instead of every January, on the same 227 months.

January: 8.5% a year, Sharpe 0.65, worst loss −22.6%. October: 7.6%, 0.57 and −31.4%. The idea still works in October, so it does not hang on one date, but January does better and uses the full calendar year of data.
